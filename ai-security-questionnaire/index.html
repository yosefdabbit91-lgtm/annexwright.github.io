The AI questions in enterprise security questionnaires — and what each answer has to rest on

Enterprise security reviews and vendor procurement questionnaires added an AI section in 2025–26. These are the twenty questions that actually turn up, grouped the way questionnaires group them. The second column is the part that catches people out: each answer only survives follow-up if there is a document behind it.

Why the second column matters more than the first

Most vendors can improvise an answer to any single question here. The problem arrives one email later, when the reviewer asks to see the thing the answer described. "Yes, we maintain an AI inventory" becomes "please attach it." An answer without an artefact behind it reads as a claim, and claims slow deals down.

So the useful preparation is not drafting answers. It is knowing which document each answer points at, and having that document exist before the questionnaire arrives.

Two worked examples

What a defensible answer looks like when there is a document behind it:

Q. Do you maintain an inventory of AI systems?

Yes. Every AI system, including third-party models and AI features in SaaS tools, is registered in our AI System Inventory with an owner, purpose, data classes, risk tier and review date. The inventory is reviewed monthly.

Rests on: an AI system inventory register. Note the scope — third-party models and AI features inside SaaS tools are where most inventories are incomplete.

Q. How do you assess AI risk?

We maintain an AI Risk Register scored on likelihood and impact with defined treatment and acceptance thresholds, reviewed quarterly. Each Limited- or High-tier system also undergoes an AI Impact Assessment before deployment and on material change, covering prohibited-practice and high-risk screens, affected persons, harms, mitigations and residual risk.

Rests on: a scored risk register and an impact assessment. The phrase that does the work is "before deployment and on material change" — it describes a process, not a one-off.

One thing worth getting right first

Before answering anything, establish your role. A provider and a deployer of the same AI system carry different duties under the EU AI Act, and answering as the wrong one makes the rest of the questionnaire inconsistent. Under the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026), Article 50 transparency duties have applied since 2 August 2026, machine-readable marking has a grace period to 2 December 2026, and Annex III high-risk obligations were deferred to 2 December 2027.
