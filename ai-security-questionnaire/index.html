The AI questions in enterprise security questionnaires — and what each answer has to rest on

Enterprise security reviews and vendor procurement questionnaires added an AI section in 2025–26. These are the twenty questions that actually turn up, grouped the way questionnaires group them. Why the second column matters more than the first

Most vendors can improvise an answer to any single question here. The problem arrives one email later, when the reviewer asks to see the thing the answer described. "Yes, we maintain an AI inventory" becomes "please attach it." An answer without an artifact behind it reads as a claim, and claims slow deals down.

So the useful preparation is not drafting answers. It is knowing which document each answer points at, and having that document exist before the questionnaire arrives. That chain — question, answer, artifact, record — is set out in full here.

What a supported answer looks like

Three rows from AW-E17, the Enterprise AI Questionnaire Answer Bank, exactly as they ship. The fourth line in each is the part reviewers actually escalate to: the record that proves the artifact is live rather than shelved.

AW-E17 · §1 Governance
QuestionDo you have an AI governance policy approved by senior management?
Starting answerYes. Our AI Governance Policy is approved by [CEO] and reviewed annually. It defines principles, roles, a risk-tiering model and mandatory controls for all AI systems we build, buy or use, and is aligned with ISO/IEC 42001:2023.
Supporting artifactAW-E02 AI Governance Policy (signed)
Record that proves itAn approval signature with a date, and the date of the last annual review.
AW-E17 · §1 Governance
QuestionDo you maintain an inventory of AI systems?
Starting answerYes. Every AI system, including third-party models and AI features in SaaS tools, is registered in our AI System Inventory with an owner, purpose, data classes, risk tier and review date. The inventory is reviewed monthly.
Supporting artifactAW-E06 AI System Inventory Register
Record that proves itPopulated rows with named owners and review dates that are not in the past. Note the scope: third-party models and AI features inside SaaS tools are where most inventories are incomplete.
AW-E17 · §2 Risk and impact management
QuestionHow do you assess AI risk?
Starting answerWe maintain an AI Risk Register scored on likelihood and impact with defined treatment and acceptance thresholds, reviewed quarterly. Each Limited- or High-tier system also undergoes an AI Impact Assessment before deployment and on material change, covering prohibited-practice and high-risk screens, affected persons, harms, mitigations and residual risk.
Supporting artifactAW-E07 AI Risk Register · AW-E08 AI Impact Assessment
Record that proves itScored risks with treatment owners, and at least one completed impact assessment for a system actually in production.

The caution that ships with the document. AW-E17's own instruction is blunt: replace bracketed facts with your reality — never claim a control you do not operate. A starting answer is a pattern to edit, not a statement of fact about your company. An answer that overstates what you do is worse than an honest gap, because the follow-up request is where it fails.
