What "certification ready" honestly means

Documentation is necessary and it is not sufficient. A certification audit looks for a management system that has been operating: a completed internal audit, a management review that actually happened, corrective actions with dates, records that accumulated over time. Professional gives you the documents and the structure to generate those records. It cannot give you the elapsed time, and no document set makes an organization certified.

What you still do. Annexwright supplies the documentation architecture. You decide which systems are in scope, who owns them, what your risk appetite is and what you will actually do; you fill the registers, adapt the wording, operate the controls and keep the records. Buying documentation does not make an organization compliant or certified.

Common questions

Will this get us certified?
It gives you the documentation an auditor expects and the structure to produce the evidence. It cannot give you the records that only elapsed time produces. The 90-day roadmap plans exactly that sequence and is candid about why first attempts usually fail.
We already have ISO/IEC 27001 — is this redundant?
No, and you are the easiest case. The management-system clauses are structurally aligned, so much of what you already run carries over; what is genuinely new is AI-specific — impact assessment, data provenance, human oversight, transparency, intended-use control. The comparison page sets out what transfers, and the crosswalk workbook is free.
Is the Statement of Applicability really complete?
The workbook ships with 38 Annex A control rows spanning A.2.2 to A.10.4, each with a justification column and an applicability decision you make. The control text is summarised in our own words; you need a licensed copy of ISO/IEC 42001:2023 for the normative text.
Can I use it with clients?
No. Professional is licensed for one organization's internal use. Consultant is the client-delivery licence.
