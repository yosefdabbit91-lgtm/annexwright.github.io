ISO/IEC 42001 × NIST AI RMF × EU AI Act × ISO/IEC 27001 crosswalk

A control-by-control mapping of ISO/IEC 42001:2023 clauses and Annex A controls against NIST AI RMF 1.0, the EU AI Act, and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — so you can see how much of an existing information security programme already carries over, and where it genuinely does not.

Indicative mapping — verify against the source texts. Satisfying a NIST subcategory does not automatically satisfy an ISO control, and the EU AI Act imposes legal duties that no standard fully discharges. Where a row says there is no direct equivalent, that is a real gap you must close separately. This is not legal advice.

Built September 2026, after the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) came into force on 27 July 2026 and moved the dates: Article 50 transparency applicable from 2 August 2026, machine-readable marking grace to 2 December 2026, Annex III high-risk deferred to 2 December 2027, Annex I to 2 August 2028. Material written before August 2026 states superseded deadlines.

How to use it

Start from the ISO/IEC 42001 column. For each clause or Annex A control, check what you already hold under ISO/IEC 27001 — for many organisations a substantial part of clauses 4 to 10 maps across, because the management-system structure is shared. What does not map across is usually the AI-specific substance: impact assessment, data governance for training data, human oversight, and the transparency duties under Article 50.

The EU AI Act column tells you which obligations attach, but your role matters more than the mapping: a provider and a deployer of the same system carry different duties. Determine that first.
